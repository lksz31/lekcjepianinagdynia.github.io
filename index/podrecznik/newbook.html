Chapter 1

Meet the Keyboard

The piano keyboard is your map. Every key has a name, a sound, and a place in music. Let's explore it together.

A standard piano has 88 keys — 52 white and 36 black. The black keys are arranged in groups of 2 and 3, which is how you navigate the instrument without looking.

Tip: Find the group of two black keys in the middle of your keyboard. The white key just to the left of them is always C — your anchor note.

Key Names

The white keys follow the musical alphabet: A B C D E F G — then it repeats. The black keys have two names each (like C# / D♭) because they sit between two white notes.

Notice the pattern: there's no black key between E and F, and none between B and C. This natural gap is what gives the piano its distinctive look.

Reading Notes

Sheet music is a visual language. Once you know a few symbols, you can decode any piece of music ever written.

The Staff

Music is written on five horizontal lines called the staff. Notes sit on the lines or in the spaces between them. Their position tells you which pitch to play.

Chapter 3

Building Chords

A chord is three or more notes played at the same time. Chords give music its emotional colour — from joyful to melancholic.

Most chords are built by stacking notes in thirds — skipping every other note of the scale. The most important chords are major (bright, happy) and minor (darker, introspective).

Major vs Minor

The difference between major and minor chords is just one note. In a C major chord you play C – E – G. Lower the E by one semitone (half-step) and you get C minor: C – E♭ – G. That tiny shift changes the entire mood.

Chapter 4

Scales & Patterns

Scales are the building blocks of melody. Master a few scales and your fingers will naturally find the right notes.

The C Major Scale

The C major scale uses only white keys: C D E F G A B C. It follows a specific pattern of whole steps (W) and half steps (H): W W H W W W H.

Click any note to hear it · Click the root (C) to play the full scale

The A Natural Minor Scale

The natural minor scale has a darker quality. Starting on A: A B C D E F G A. It follows the pattern: W H W W H W W.

Why Practice Scales?

Scales build finger independence, train your ear to recognise intervals, and give you a mental map of the keyboard. Even 5 minutes of scales per day creates noticeable progress within weeks.

Practice tip: Start slowly — much slower than you think necessary. Speed comes naturally once your fingers know the pattern. Use a metronome set to 60 BPM and increase by 4 each week.

Chapter 5

Rhythm & Time

Without rhythm, notes are just sounds. Rhythm is the heartbeat of music — it's what makes you tap your foot.

Most music is grouped into measures (or bars). A 4/4 time signature means 4 beats per measure, with the quarter note getting one beat. This is the most common time signature in Western music.
